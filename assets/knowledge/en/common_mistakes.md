# 8 Common Mistakes That Artificially Inflate Blood Pressure

Small errors in measurement preparation or posture can artificially inflate your reading, leading to misdiagnosis or unnecessary medication adjustments. The **American Heart Association (AHA)** highlights the following common mistakes:

## 1. Full Bladder (+10 to 15 mmHg)
Holding your urine causes sympathetic nervous system stimulation, driving pressure up. Always empty your bladder before checking.

## 2. Slumping or Lack of Back Support (+6 to 10 mmHg)
Sitting on an exam table or couch without back support strains core muscles and raises diastolic and systolic numbers. Use a supportive chair.

## 3. Crossing Legs or Feet (+2 to 8 mmHg)
Crossing legs at the knees or ankles restricts blood circulation and artificially increases venous return, elevating pressure. Keep both feet flat on the ground.

## 4. Unsupported Arm (+10 mmHg)
If your arm hangs down or you use your muscles to hold it up, isometric muscle contraction immediately elevates blood pressure. Rest the arm comfortably on a desk or table.

## 5. Cuff Over Thick Clothing (+5 to 50 mmHg)
Placing the cuff over a sweater or rolling up a tight sleeve (which acts like a tourniquet) distorts sensor accuracy. Place the cuff directly against bare skin or a very thin shirt.

## 6. Incorrect Cuff Size (+2 to 10 mmHg)
- **Too small / too tight**: Significantly overestimates blood pressure.
- **Too large / loose**: Underestimates blood pressure.
Measure your upper arm circumference and choose the proper cuff size (Small, Standard/Adult, or Large Adult).

## 7. Talking or Texting (+10 mmHg)
Talking, active listening, or looking at stimulating phone content elevates stress hormones. Remain quietly still during the entire measurement cycle.

## 8. Inadequate Rest Time (+10 to 20 mmHg)
Taking a reading immediately after climbing stairs, carrying groceries, or experiencing a heated discussion reflects exertion, not resting baseline. Rest quietly for 5 minutes first.
