# Sodium Reduction and the DASH Diet: Food as Medicine

When it comes to non-pharmacological blood pressure reduction, dietary modifications have clinical efficacy comparable to a single antihypertensive medication.

## The AHA Sodium Recommendations

The **American Heart Association (AHA)** guidelines advise:
- **General Adult Ceiling**: No more than **2,300 milligrams (mg)** of sodium per day (roughly 1 teaspoon of table salt).
- **Optimal Target**: Cutting back to an ideal limit of **1,500 mg per day** can lower systolic blood pressure by up to 5 to 6 mmHg in hypertensive individuals.

The average American consumes over **3,400 mg** of sodium daily—more than double the ideal target.

## Where Sodium Lurks: The "Salty Six"

According to the AHA, over 70% of dietary sodium comes from packaged, prepared, and restaurant foods, not the kitchen salt shaker. Watch out for the "Salty Six":
1. **Breads and rolls** (a major silent source)
2. **Pizza** (cheese, crust, and cured meats)
3. **Sandwiches and burgers** (condiments and deli meats)
4. **Cold cuts and cured meats** (bacon, salami, ham, turkey slices)
5. **Canned soups and broths**
6. **Burritos and tacos**

## The DASH Eating Plan (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension)

Developed by the U.S. National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI):
- **Rich in**: Fruits, vegetables, whole grains, beans, nuts, and fat-free/low-fat dairy.
- **Low in**: Saturated fats, full-fat dairy, tropical oils, and sugar-sweetened beverages.
- **High in Potassium, Magnesium, & Calcium**: Potassium counteracts sodium by relaxing vascular walls and promoting sodium excretion via urine.

> 🥗 Combining the DASH diet with sodium reduction can reduce systolic blood pressure by **11 mmHg or more**.
