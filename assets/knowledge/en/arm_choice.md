# Left Arm or Right Arm? Choosing the Measurement Arm

A common question among patients is which arm should be measured when checking blood pressure at home. Here is the consensus guidance from the **American Heart Association (AHA)**.

## The First Assessment: Measure Both Arms

When first starting home blood pressure monitoring, measure your blood pressure in **both arms**:

1. Take a reading on the left arm, rest for 1–2 minutes, and take a reading on the right arm.
2. Repeat once more to verify accuracy.
3. Determine if one arm consistently records a higher number.

## The Rule of the Higher Reading

- An inter-arm difference of **5 to 10 mmHg** is common and normal.
- **Always use the arm that gives the higher reading** for all subsequent daily tracking. 
- Clinical decisions and diagnostic thresholds are based on the higher reading.

## When to Speak with Your Physician

- If there is a consistent difference of **more than 10 mmHg** in systolic pressure between both arms, notify your doctor.
- A consistent difference exceeding **15 to 20 mmHg** may indicate arterial narrowing (peripheral artery disease, subclavian stenosis) and requires medical evaluation.

> 💡 Consistency is key. Once your physician confirms which arm to use, stick to that arm for all routine monitoring.
