# Systolic, Diastolic, and Pulse: What Do the Numbers Mean?

Understanding your blood pressure numbers is the cornerstone of proactive cardiovascular health. Every measurement provides three crucial metrics.

## Systolic Blood Pressure (The Top Number)

Systolic pressure measures the force your heart exerts on the walls of your arteries each time it **contracts (beats)**.

- **Primary focus after age 50**: Systolic pressure naturally tends to rise with age due to progressive stiffening of large arteries and long-term plaque buildup.
- Major risk predictor for stroke, heart attack, and heart failure.

## Diastolic Blood Pressure (The Bottom Number)

Diastolic pressure measures the pressure in the vessels when your heart is **at rest between beats**.

- Reflects total peripheral vascular resistance.
- Frequently elevated in young and middle-aged adults with early hypertension, often linked to elevated stress, high sodium intake, or physical deconditioning.

## Pulse Pressure (Systolic minus Diastolic)

- A normal pulse pressure is approximately **30 to 50 mmHg**.
- A widened pulse pressure (>60 mmHg) often indicates marked arterial stiffness, atherosclerosis, or leaky aortic valves, especially in older adults.

## Pulse Rate / Heart Rate (BPM)

Your monitor measures the number of arterial beats per minute.

- Normal resting adult heart rate typically ranges between **60 and 100 BPM**.
- Well-trained endurance athletes may exhibit resting heart rates in the 40s or 50s without negative symptoms.
- Irregular pulse warnings on your device may signal arrhythmias like atrial fibrillation and warrant medical follow-up.

> ⚠️ Guidance provided here is educational and based on American Heart Association materials. Always consult your personal physician for clinical diagnosis and treatment.
