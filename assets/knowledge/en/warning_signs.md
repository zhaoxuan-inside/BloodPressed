# Warning Signs: When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

While hypertension is commonly referred to as the "silent killer" because it typically produces no everyday symptoms, severe acute elevations can cause life-threatening end-organ damage. Know when to call emergency services.

## Hypertensive Crisis: Over 180 and/or Over 120

According to the **American Heart Association (AHA)**:
- If your reading suddenly exceeds **180 systolic AND/OR 120 diastolic**:
  1. Wait **5 minutes** in a calm environment.
  2. Take your reading a second time.
  3. If your reading remains at or above 180/120 mmHg without other symptoms, contact your doctor promptly for urgent advice (Hypertensive Urgency).

## Red Flag Symptoms (Hypertensive Emergency - Call 911 Immediately)

If your reading exceeds 180/120 mmHg AND you experience **ANY** of the following warning signs, **DO NOT WAIT**. Call 911 or visit the nearest emergency department immediately:

- **Chest pain or pressure** (angina / suspected heart attack)
- **Shortness of breath** or difficulty catching your breath
- **Severe, sudden headache** (unlike any typical headache)
- **Numbness, weakness, or facial drooping** on one side of the body
- **Difficulty speaking**, slurred speech, or confusion
- **Sudden vision changes** (blurriness, double vision, partial blindness)
- **Severe upper back pain** (tearing sensation, possible aortic dissection)
- **Seizures or unresponsiveness**

## Critical Precaution

> ⚠️ **Do not attempt to rapidly lower extremely high blood pressure yourself** by taking double doses of medication without explicit emergency physician instructions. Precipitous drops in cerebral perfusion can trigger ischemic strokes. Seek professional emergency care immediately.
