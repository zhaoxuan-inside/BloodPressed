# Home vs. Clinic Readings: Why the Targets Differ

You may notice that while clinical guidelines define Stage 1 Hypertension in the office starting at 130/80 mmHg, home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) thresholds and clinic readings often differ.

## The Clinic vs. Home Discrepancy

Office blood pressure readings are performed by medical staff, often under time constraints, clinical anxiety, or rushed schedules. In contrast, home measurements reflect your actual resting daily state:

- **White Coat Effect**: Nearly 20% to 30% of patients experience anxiety in a medical clinic that temporarily spikes their systolic pressure by 10 to 30 mmHg.
- **Masked Hypertension**: Conversely, some patients show normal readings in the physician's office but dangerous elevations during work, daily life, or sleep.

## Target Threshold Comparison (AHA / ACC Consensus)

| Setting | Normal Baseline | Hypertension Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **Office / Clinic** | <120 / <80 | ≥130 / ≥80 (Stage 1) |
| **Home Self-Monitoring (HBPM)** | <120 / <80 | Average ≥130 / ≥80 |
| **24-Hour Ambulatory (ABPM Average)** | <115 / <75 | ≥125 / ≥75 |
| **Daytime Ambulatory** | <120 / <80 | ≥130 / ≥80 |
| **Nighttime Ambulatory** | <100 / <65 | ≥110 / ≥65 |

## Why Physicians Trust Home Monitoring

The **American Heart Association** recommends home monitoring for nearly all adults diagnosed with or suspected of having high blood pressure. Home readings:
1. Provide dozens of data points over weeks rather than a single rushed snapshot.
2. Accurately verify whether your prescription drugs are working all day long.
3. Strongly correlate with long-term cardiovascular outcomes and stroke prevention.
