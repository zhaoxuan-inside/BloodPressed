# The Proper Routine for Home BP Monitoring: Frequency & Logging

Home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) provides vital data that clinic visits simply cannot replicate. However, sporadic or obsessive measuring can lead to misleading data or measurement anxiety. Here is the protocol endorsed by the **American Heart Association (AHA)**.

## The 7-Day Diagnostic & Follow-Up Protocol

Whenever your medication regimen changes, or during the week leading up to a doctor's consultation, follow the **Rule of Twos**:

- **Morning**: Take 2 readings, 1 minute apart, after waking, emptying your bladder, and sitting quietly for 5 minutes (before breakfast and morning medications).
- **Evening**: Take 2 readings, 1 minute apart, before dinner or before going to bed.
- **Duration**: Repeat for **7 consecutive days** (minimum 3–4 days). Discard day 1 readings and average the remaining days to obtain your true clinical baseline.

## Routine Maintenance Monitoring

Once your blood pressure is well-controlled:
- Measuring **1 to 2 days per week** (morning and evening pairs) is typically sufficient.
- Avoid measuring 10 times a day in response to transient anxiety, headache, or mild stress; momentary spikes are normal physiological reactions and obsessing over them creates a feedback loop of elevated readings.

## Key Log Information to Share with Your Doctor

A complete digital log (like the export feature in BloodPressed) should record:
1. Exact timestamp and date
2. Systolic, Diastolic, and Pulse rate
3. Arm measured (Left vs. Right)
4. Context notes (e.g., missed dose, intense workday, poor sleep, or caffeine consumption)
