# Exercise and Physical Activity: Natural Blood Pressure Therapy

Regular physical activity strengthens your heart muscle, allowing it to pump more blood with less strain and lowering arterial resistance. The **American Heart Association (AHA)** and the **U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS)** recommend the following exercise prescription.

## The HHS / AHA Exercise Prescription

To achieve clinically meaningful blood pressure reduction (an average drop of **5 to 8 mmHg**):

1. **Aerobic Activity**:
   - At least **150 minutes per week** of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity (e.g., 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week), OR
   - **75 minutes per week** of vigorous aerobic activity (e.g., running, lap swimming), or an equivalent combination.
2. **Muscle-Strengthening (Resistance Training)**:
   - Moderate-to-high intensity muscle-strengthening activity involving all major muscle groups at least **2 days per week**.
3. **Isometric Resistance Training (Emerging Evidence)**:
   - Isometric handgrip squeezes or wall squats (isometric squats) for 2–4 sets of 2 minutes, 3 times per week, have shown substantial blood pressure-lowering effects in clinical trials.

## Defining Moderate Intensity

You are working at moderate intensity if:
- Your breathing is noticeably faster, but you can still **talk comfortably in complete sentences** (though you cannot sing).
- You are lightly sweating after 10 minutes.
- Examples: Brisk walking (≥3 mph), casual cycling, water aerobics, doubles tennis, dancing.

## A Beginner's 4-Week Progression

| Week | Target Activity |
|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Two 10-minute brisk walks per day, 5 days/week |
| **Week 2** | One 20-minute continuous brisk walk, 5 days/week |
| **Week 3** | 25 minutes of brisk walking or cycling, 5 days/week |
| **Week 4** | 30 minutes of aerobic exercise, 5 days/week + 2 days of bodyweight resistance |

> ⚠️ If your blood pressure is currently uncontrolled (≥180/120 mmHg), consult your doctor before initiating new vigorous exercise routines.
